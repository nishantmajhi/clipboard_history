3 Paragraph

Clipboard managers exist because most workflows involve gathering pieces of information from many different places before you can act on any of them. Instead of switching back and forth between a source and a destination document, a good clipboard history tool lets you copy everything first, in whatever order it's convenient to find it, and then assemble and format it all in one pass at the end.

A regular web page has no way to attach Windows' "exclude from clipboard history" tag to a copy — only native apps (password managers, browser autofill, etc.) that specifically opt in can do that. So these three will show up in the .txt log just like anything else, in plain text. That's expected, and it's exactly the scenario the README warns about.
